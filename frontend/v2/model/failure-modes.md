# SoftFocus v2 — Failure Modes & Misinterpretation Risks

**Status:** DESIGN-ONLY  
**Phase:** Phase C — Analytical Consolidation  
**Scope:** Risk analysis  
**Last updated:** 2026-02-11

---

## Purpose

This document enumerates **known failure modes**
and **interpretive risks** inherent to the v2 model.

Its goal is to prevent:
- misuse
- oversimplification
- clinical drift
- false reassurance or alarm

---

## Failure Modes

### 1. Single-Dimension Reading

**Risk:** Interpreting Dimension A, B, or C in isolation.

**Consequence:** False conclusions about stability or risk.

**Mitigation:** Enforce cross-dimension context in all narratives.

---

### 2. Control Illusion Normalization

**Risk:** Treating high B as competence or resilience.

**Consequence:** Masked fragmentation and delayed recognition.

**Mitigation:** Always interpret B relative to C.

---

### 3. Fragmentation as Weakness

**Risk:** Reading Dimension C as personal failure.

**Consequence:** Moralization of structural effects.

**Mitigation:** Emphasize downstream, non-volitional nature of C.

---

### 4. Over-Pathologization

**Risk:** Treating high density as diagnosis.

**Consequence:** Clinical drift, anxiety amplification.

**Mitigation:** Strict non-clinical language and framing.

---

### 5. False Safety From Low A

**Risk:** Assuming stable environment implies low risk.

**Consequence:** Silent fragmentation overlooked.

**Mitigation:** Highlight Silent Fragmentation pattern explicitly.

---

### 6. Temporal Misreading

**Risk:** Applying v2 to short-term spikes.

**Consequence:** Overreaction to transient states.

**Mitigation:** Reinforce 3–4 week window requirement.

---

## Design Implications

Any frontend or narrative surface must:

- prevent single-score collapse
- avoid labels implying diagnosis
- resist optimization framing
- support exit after understanding

---

## Final Constraint

If v2 interpretation ever answers:
“What should I do next?”

the model has been misapplied.

v2 exists to provide **orientation**, not direction.
